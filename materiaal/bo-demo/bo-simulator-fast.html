Simulator Tutorial

The Challenge: Finding the Hidden Optimum

This simulation demonstrates how Bayesian Optimization (BO) intelligently finds the best location for a Distribution Center (DC). The goal is to find the location with the absolute lowest total cost—the lowest valley on the map—by running as few expensive "feasibility studies" (tests) as possible.

Crucially, the "best" location isn't obvious! We've designed the cost to include a hidden "low-cost zone" away from the center, making this a challenging, realistic problem where random guessing would fail.

The 3D View Explained

  • Grey Cylinders: Your customer locations. Distance to these is a major cost factor.
  • Surfaces (Toggle them in Controls):
    • True Cost (Black Wireframe): This is the real, hidden cost landscape. In the real world, this is what you're trying to discover. The lowest valley is the true best answer. Notice how it's not in the center of the customers!
    • Belief (Shaded Surface): This is the AI's "Smart Predictor"—its best guess about the cost landscape based on the tests so far. Its height is the predicted cost. Pale means it is very uncertain about that area; darker grey means it is more certain.
    • Decision (Red Surface): This is the AI's "Smart Decision Maker." It shows where it would be most valuable to test next. The lowest point marks the best balance of expected low cost (exploitation) and high uncertainty (exploration).
  • Markers:
    • Black Spheres: Locations where a test (feasibility study) has already been run.
    • Yellow Sphere: The best location found so far among all the tests.
    • Red Sphere: The location the AI has decided to test next.

How to Use It

1. Initialize / Reset: Starts the simulation. It runs two random tests to give the AI its first clues.

2. Run One Step: Click this to watch the AI perform one cycle: It updates its belief, uses the purple decision surface to pick the next best spot (red marker), and runs a new test.

3. Run Automatically: Let the simulation run on its own up to the "Max Auto Runs" limit.

4. Experiment with Parameters:

  • Observation Noise: Simulates real-world measurement errors. At 0.00, every test is perfect and black spheres will land exactly on the black wireframe. At higher values, test results are less reliable, making the problem harder.
  • Exploration (Beta): This is the core strategy control. A high Beta makes the AI adventurous, prioritizing exploring uncertain (pale) areas. A low Beta makes it conservative, focusing only on exploiting areas it already thinks are good (the lowest valleys).
  • Graphics Quality: Lower this for smoother performance on less powerful computers. It doesn't affect the math.
  • Max Auto Runs: Sets the number of iterations for the "Run Automatically" mode.
